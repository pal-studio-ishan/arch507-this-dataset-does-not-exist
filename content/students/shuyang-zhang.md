## The question
Jesseco didn't start from a style but from a question: can AI act as a design partner that brings together things that rarely coexist, like the ancient and the futuristic, the sacred and the technological, and turn them into scenes that still make sense?

## What they did
- Built a dataset held together not by one look but by shared principles: monumental scale, axial composition, ceremonial space, celestial backdrops, technology intruding, and painterly light.
- Designed a two-level workflow. First a language model (GPT) turns a rough idea, mood and sketch into a structured prompt covering composition, hierarchy and key objects. Then Flux with the LoRA renders it.
- Made twelve "Contrast" images, from a temple plaza under giant planets to 1950s American suburbs with flying saucers and a medieval village under a ringed planet.

## What they found
- Some images held the tension between ancient order and future imagery, but results weren't always stable.
- Often the model fell back on familiar dramatic motifs, giving images that were striking but not spatially new.
- Coherence doesn't automatically mean originality. The value was in opening up possibilities to evaluate and question, not in final answers.
